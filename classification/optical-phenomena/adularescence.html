Adularescence (Moonstone Sheen) ​

Overview ​

The signature effect of moonstone (feldspar family). On a cabochon cut, the gem displays a soft, floating blue-to-white sheen that seems to drift as the stone is rotated — like moonlight on water. Mechanism: alternating thin lamellae of orthoclase and albite (~100–300 nm each). The refractive-index mismatch between layers scatters and interferes with light. This is a structural optical effect, not related to color centers or chromophore elements.

Mechanism ​

Lamellar scattering + interference: alternating KAlSi₃O₈ (orthoclase) and NaAlSi₃O₈ (albite) lamellae with thickness on the order of visible light wavelengths. Light scattered across layers interferes coherently; blue light (shorter wavelength) matches the layer spacing for constructive interference, producing the blue/silver sheen. Orange and red (longer wavelengths) interfere destructively, so the effect is always blue-white.
