# Rough Planning & Orientation

## Overview

Cutting does not begin with choosing an attractive outline. The cutter first reads the
rough: color zoning, fractures, inclusions, transparency, crystallographic direction,
and possible optical phenomena. A plan balances weight retention, appearance, and
durability, recording the table orientation, target outline, risks, and acceptable loss.


## Core Principles

- Inspect before sawing: clean, measure, and examine the rough under different lights and directions; mark fractures, inclusions, and color bands.
- Prioritize orientation: with zoning, chatoyancy, asterism, or play of color, table direction controls how the effect is seen.
- Make trade-offs explicit: retaining weight does not guarantee value; chasing weight can sacrifice brightness, symmetry, or durability.
- Reserve a safety margin: avoid or reinforce corners, thin girdles, deep fractures, and stress concentrations in the design stage.
- The final plan should explain why the stone was cut that way, not only describe its finished outline.

## Examples

| Gem | Cut | Note |
|---|---|---|
| opal | orient the face toward the strongest play of color | prioritise the strongest color patches |
| star sapphire | align the dome with the intersecting silk | centre the star at the dome |
