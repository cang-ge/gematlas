# Fancy Cuts

## Overview

Many non-round outlines are commonly grouped as fancy cuts, depending on the
faceting pattern and trade usage. Examples include princess, emerald step, pear,
oval, marquise, and heart; choice follows rough shape, inclusion placement, and taste.

## Case Flow

> Teaching case (demonstration): a market judgement cannot be derived from an outline name alone.

### 1. Define the goal

State whether the goal is rough utilisation, outline character, corner durability, or visual performance.
Different goals change tolerance for length-to-width ratio, corner treatment, and loss.

### 2. Compare outline and faceting

Compare pear, marquise, or emerald-step candidates against the same rough. Record length-to-width ratio,
symmetry, corners, step/brilliant facet organisation, and possible bow-tie dark zones. Outline is only the entry point;
it does not replace proportion or polish review.

### 3. Review the visual result

Rotate the stone under consistent light and record uniformity, overly thin tips, and left-right balance. Keep
observations from different angles separate instead of treating one view as the whole result.

### 4. Stop conditions

If bow-tie behaviour, corner durability, or symmetry cannot be judged from the available evidence, keep multiple
candidates open. Do not write “looks better” as a certain conclusion or infer price directly.


## Core Principles

- Princess: square, retains round-brilliant fire; modern bridal staple.
- Emerald: step facets, showcases clarity — suited to clean large stones.
- Pear / marquise / heart: elongated silhouettes; need symmetric crown-pavilion to avoid bow-tie.
- Cut quality, outline, proportions, symmetry, and polish jointly affect appearance and market assessment; value still varies by species and market.
