# Coloured-Stone Grading

## Overview

Coloured gems lack a single 4C scheme. Instead, colour is graded on
three axes—hue (position on the colour wheel), tone (lightness), and
saturation (intensity). Many laboratories use these descriptive dimensions,
but report terms and thresholds can differ.

## Core Principles

- Hue: position on the colour wheel; preferred ranges vary by species and reporting system.
- Tone: lightness; some systems use named bands, but bands and preferred ranges are not universal.
- Saturation: vividness; high saturation is not a universal best condition for every species or market.
- Colour is often important, but cut, clarity, durability, treatment, and supply also affect value.

## Examples

| Gem | Grade | Note |
|---|---|---|
| ruby | vivid red, medium tone | Pigeon-blood red requires species, lab, and context |
| emerald | vivid green, slightly bluish | Muzo / Chivor may be origin or trade context, not a universal grade |
| sapphire | vivid blue, medium-dark | Royal / cornflower require a stated report system and scope |
