# Synthetics & Imitations

## Overview

Record at least four separate dimensions: natural or laboratory-grown, treated or not,
a different-material imitation, and any doublet/triplet or composite structure.
Synthetics, imitations, treated materials, and composites require different evidence paths;
typical clues support candidates but do not replace laboratory disclosure.

## Identification Steps

**Four-dimensional disclosure protocol**

| Dimension | Question | Recorded output |
|---|---|---|
| Material identity | What material or mineral is it? | Candidate, supporting/conflicting evidence, unresolved items |
| Growth origin | Natural formation or laboratory growth? | Growth clues, observation conditions, unresolved items |
| Treatment status | Could it be heated, filled, dyed, irradiated, or otherwise treated? | Treatment clues, unverified items, disclosure risk |
| Structure disclosure | Is it an imitation, doublet, triplet, or composite? | Junctions, material clues, and accessible areas |

Keep the four dimensions separate. “Emerald” does not mean “natural emerald”; “laboratory-grown” does not mean “untreated”; and “imitation” describes a material relationship, not quality or value.

**Evidence-layer matrix**

| Evidence layer | Can support | Cannot establish alone | Record |
|---|---|---|---|
| Appearance/photos | Candidate formation and visible anomalies | Natural, synthetic, treatment, or origin | Light, background, mounting |
| Microscopy | Inclusion, growth, or junction clues | Absence does not exclude a category | Magnification, light, direction, image ID |
| RI/SG/optical reactions | Narrowing a material candidate | Natural/synthetic disclosure alone | Instrument, quality, repeats |
| Spectroscopy/advanced instruments | Structural, growth, or treatment clues | Final conclusions without calibration/workflow | Instrument, calibration, optical path, raw file |
| Laboratory report | Formal identity and disclosure opinion | Claims outside the report scope | Report ID, scope, limitations |

**How to use clues correctly**

Curved growth lines, bubbles, platinum-like inclusions, flow lines, unusual dispersion, UV zoning, and spectral patterns are possible supporting clues. Record magnification, light, direction, instrument, and repeats. Not seeing a feature means it was not observed under this setup; it does not prove natural origin or exclude synthesis.

**Scenario matrix: ask the question before selecting evidence**

| Scenario | Primary question | Educational next step | Escalation signal |
|---|---|---|---|
| CVD/HPHT diamond | Is the material diamond, and can growth origin be disclosed? | Record visual/microscopy clues and check instrument suitability | Natural/laboratory-grown or formal disclosure needed |
| Synthetic corundum | Is it corundum, and are there laboratory-growth clues? | Cross-check RI, optical behavior, microscopy, and spectra | Overlapping clues or unclear treatment |
| Hydrothermal emerald | Can material identity and growth method be separated? | Record inclusions, optics, and equipment conditions | Natural/synthetic or treatment disclosure needed |
| Glass / cubic zirconia | Is it an imitation made from a different material? | Record structure and suitable physical clues | Mounted, composite, or conflicting results |
| Doublet / triplet | Is there a junction between different materials? | Use low-risk observation and mark inaccessible areas | Any formal structure or transaction disclosure |

**Recommended workflow**

1. Define the task: educational screening, material candidate, or formal transaction/disclosure.
2. Record the specimen: photos, mounting, size, accessible surfaces, existing damage, and suspected composite structure.
3. Start with low-risk observation, then choose suitable physical, optical, or instrument evidence; do not add destructive tests just to confirm a hypothesis.
4. Label each clue as “supports candidate”, “consistent with candidate”, “cannot exclude”, or “unreliable”, preserving conditions and raw records.
5. Conclude separately across the four disclosure dimensions; write “pending verification” when evidence is missing.
6. Stop and refer to a qualified laboratory when results conflict, the specimen is inaccessible, value is high, or formal disclosure is required.

**Stop conditions**

Do not turn “looks like”, “no anomaly observed”, or “one value is close” into a natural/synthetic conclusion. No fixed test set is sufficient for every CVD/HPHT, synthetic corundum, hydrothermal emerald, glass, cubic zirconia, or composite specimen.


## Core Principles

- Natural, treated, synthetic, imitation, and composite are separate disclosure dimensions, not one true/false label.
- Growth lines, bubbles, platinum-like inclusions, flow lines, unusual dispersion, UV zoning, and spectra are possible clues, not mandatory features.
- CVD/HPHT, synthetic corundum, and hydrothermal emerald require cross-testing chosen for the sample and equipment; no universal two-test shortcut is sufficient.
- Typical SG, RI, or visual differences need conditions and sources; one value, facet edge, or “glassy” appearance cannot independently exclude a candidate.

## Examples

| Gem | Value | Note |
|---|---|---|
| diamond | cubic zirconia (CZ) | Different-material imitation candidate; combine testing with formal disclosure |
| emerald | hydrothermal synthetic | Growth origin needs laboratory verification |
