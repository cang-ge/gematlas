# Identification

Identification uses recorded, reproducible physical and optical evidence to narrow a material
candidate. This module supports observation, basic screening, and evidence documentation; it does
not turn appearance or one reading into a certain answer. Follow this path: record the sample and
scope, start with low-risk observation, escalate physical and optical tests by risk, distinguish
“supports a candidate”, “consistent with a candidate”, and “cannot exclude”, then refer to a lab when needed.

**What this module can do:** teach observation, test-condition logging, and the role of RI / SG / optical
observations in an evidence chain, producing an educational screening record.

**What it cannot do:** a photo, appearance, single reading, or Assist response cannot establish a specific
sample's natural/synthetic identity, treatment, origin, grade, or value. Preserve raw records and refer high-value,
insurance, origin, or disclosure decisions to a qualified laboratory.


## Identification Topics

| Topic | Summary |
|---|---|
| [Identification Workflow & Documentation](identification-workflow) | Professional identification is not a search for one “magic reading”; it is a reproducible chain of evidence. Record the sample first, begin with low-risk observations, combine physical, optical, and inclusion clues, then state the confidence limits, treatment disclosure, and whether a laboratory report is needed. |
| [Physical Tests](physical-tests) | Refractive index, specific gravity, hardness, and cleavage are the most basic identification tools — influenced by chemistry and crystal structure, so each species tends to fall within a comparable range; conditions, orientation, treatment, and sample state can shift the reading. |
| [Optical Tests](optical-tests) | Birefringence (polariscope), pleochroism (dichroscope), and UV fluorescence (UV lamp) provide optical and material clues — useful for screening species and deciding what to test next. |
| [Synthetics & Imitations](synthetic-and-imitation) | Record at least four separate dimensions: natural or laboratory-grown, treated or not, a different-material imitation, and any doublet/triplet or composite structure. Synthetics, imitations, treated materials, and composites require different evidence paths; typical clues support candidates but do not replace laboratory disclosure. |
| [Same-Colour Gems](same-color-gems) | One colour can hide many species — red may be ruby, spinel, garnet, or glass. Combining RI / SG / pleochroism / inclusions narrows the candidate set, but overlap and unresolved results are normal. The red case is expanded alongside minimal blue and green educational branches; none is a complete decision tree for every material. |
