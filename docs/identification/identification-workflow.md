# Identification Workflow & Documentation

## Overview

Professional identification is not a search for one “magic reading”; it is a
reproducible chain of evidence. Record the sample first, begin with low-risk
observations, combine physical, optical, and inclusion clues, then state the
confidence limits, treatment disclosure, and whether a laboratory report is needed.


## Identification Steps

**1. Record the sample and scope**

Note dimensions, weight, mounting, known provenance, visible damage, and the purpose of the
examination. Record which tests are unreliable or unavailable for mounted, oiled, or inaccessible samples.

**2. Start with non-destructive observation**

Under white light, a point source, and suitable magnification, record body color, transparency,
luster, cut, inclusions, and phenomena. Write observations before candidate names.

**3. Escalate tests by risk**

Prefer refractive index, polariscope, dichroscope, UV fluorescence, and spectroscopy as non-destructive
clues. Assess whether a specific-gravity liquid test is suitable; scratch testing requires a clear need and permission.

**4. Cross-check and grade the conclusion**

Compare readings with candidate ranges, treatment possibilities, and sample limitations. Distinguish
“supports a candidate”, “consistent with a candidate”, and “cannot exclude”; a single test is not a final ID.

**5. Refer to a laboratory when needed**

For natural-versus-synthetic decisions, origin opinions, major transactions, or insurance, preserve raw
observations and refer the sample to a qualified laboratory for advanced testing.


## Core Principles

- Keep separate fields for “what was observed” and “what it suggests” so observation and conclusion do not contaminate each other.
- Record test conditions, orientation, contact state, and repeated readings; values cannot be compared without context.
- Keep the conclusion weaker than the evidence: a screen is not a certificate, and an origin opinion is not a grade.
- Confirm permission, sample value, and safety conditions before any destructive test.
