Labradorescence (Spectrolite) ​

Overview ​

The signature effect of labradorite (plagioclase feldspar series). On a cabochon cut, alternating nanoscale layers or compositional striations reflect and interfere with light, producing vivid flashes of blue, green, orange, and gold with a metallic sheen. The colors shift continuously with viewing angle — a holographic quality. The mechanism is similar to adularescence but with greater compositional variation and refractive-index contrast between layers, producing more saturated colors. The most famous example is Finnish Spectrolite.

Mechanism ​

Compositional lamellar structure: labradorite's anorthite (CaAl₂Si₂O₈) and albite (NaAlSi₃O₈) sub-layers alternate with thicknesses of ~50–250 nm. Refractive-index contrasts between layers (typically 0.005–0.012) fall in the range needed for strong selective interference with visible light. As viewing angle changes, the reflected wavelength shifts, producing angle-dependent color variation.
